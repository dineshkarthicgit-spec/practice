
1. Resistor

• What it does: Limits or restricts the flow of electric current in a circuit.
• Working Principle: It operates based on Ohm's Law (V = IR). Made of resistive material (such as carbon or metal film), it offers opposition to the movement of free electrons, converting a portion of the electrical energy into heat and dropping the voltage level across it.

2. Capacitor

• What it does: Stores and releases electrical energy in an electrostatic field.
• Working Principle: It consists of two conductive plates separated by an insulating material called a dielectric. When a DC voltage is applied, positive and negative charges build up on opposite plates, storing energy (Q = CV) while blocking direct current and letting alternating current pass through.

3. Inductor

• What it does: Stores energy in a magnetic field when electrical current flows through it.
• Working Principle: It is typically built as a coil of wire. According to Faraday's Law of Induction, when current changes through the coil, it induces a back-electromotive force (back-EMF) that opposes the change in current.

4. Diode

• What it does: Allows electric current to flow in only one direction.
• Working Principle: Built as a p-n junction semiconductor, it offers very low resistance when forward-biased (allowing current through) and extremely high resistance when reverse-biased (blocking current), acting like a one-way check valve for electricity.

5. Transistor

• What it does: Amplifies weak signals or acts as a fast electronic switch.
• Working Principle: Typically made as a three-terminal semiconductor (such as a BJT or MOSFET), a small current or voltage applied to the control terminal (Base or Gate) controls a much larger current flowing between the other two terminals (Emitter/Collector or Source/Drain)
